This is MVP version two. First, the home screen now explains the name: we hand the scam to the people who should stop it, not reply to the scammer. Second, a Known senders list. It is saved on the device in localStorage, and you can export it as JSON. Third, when a message comes from a saved sender, the app adds a REPEAT stamp, which adds 36 to the score, capped at 100. Fourth, a Write a follow-up button. It makes a complaint draft for Gmail, Outlook, a generic email, or the phone operator and WhatsApp, and it cites the first report date and the evidence. Finally, buttons open IASC, CekRekening and Patroli Siber. Nothing is sent automatically; the user submits it. Links: iasc.ojk.go.id, cekrekening.id, patrolisiber.id.

These are the real Wireshark captures from that run, with the MAC addresses masked. On the left is the link between the victim and the switch. Filter off: the DNS A answer, then HTTP 1.1 200 OK. Filter on: DNS says no such name, and all that is left are SYN retransmissions to 10.10.2.66 that nobody answers. On the right is the link between the gateway and the phishing server. With the filter off, the HTTP session reaches it. With the filter on, nothing from the victim arrives — the DROP rule works. This is a lab only; a real deployment would be an ISP or campus DNS filter with a TrustPositif-style blocklist.

This is the law angle, which is our group's role. Our point is the provider's duty of care. Under UU ITE Articles 15 and 16, electronic system operators must run their systems reliably and securely, and they are responsible for them. Under UU PDP, Law 27 of 2022, Articles 35, 39 and 47, the data controller must secure personal data, prevent unauthorized access, and is accountable. So a documented repeat report changes the story from "we didn't know" to "you were told". We also have a policy idea: institutions can require Chrome Enhanced Safe Browsing through the policy SafeBrowsingProtectionLevel equals 2. Google says Enhanced Protection users are phished 20 to 35 percent less. But more browsing data goes to Google, so under UU PDP the institution needs a privacy notice. Sir, you asked for a real example, so here are two published cases, not from our own inbox. First, an email. On 23 September 2024, BCA warned about a fake Customer Service email saying the customer's credit card is being misused, with a link to block it. The form looks like BCA and asks for the card number, expiry date, CVV, and then the SMS OTP. BCA says its customer service never asks for personal data, through any channel. Second, on 4 September 2025 Detik reported an iMessage saying a J&T package label was damaged: update the address within 24 hours, and pay an extra charge of about 9,000 rupiah. The page kept saying the payment failed. The victim tried five times, and every charge went through in Saudi riyal — about 70 million rupiah lost. The package was never stuck. Our lab domain models exactly this kind of lure.
